Welcome

This Handbook explains the mission and values of the Greek Orthodox Community of South Australia Inc (GOCSA) and outlines the services available to support your assessed needs at home. It also includes important information to help keep both you and our staff safe during in-home support.

Your Care Partner

A Care Partner is your main point of contact at GOCSA. They work alongside you to plan, coordinate and review your services so they meet your individual needs, preferences and goals.

Your Care Partner will:

  • Develop your care plan with you
  • Organise your services
  • Monitor the quality of your supports
  • Make adjustments as your needs change
  • Help you navigate your care and your funding

They will also work with you to help you remain living safely and independently at home for as long as possible. If you have any questions or would like more detail about anything in this Handbook, please contact your Care Partner. They are there to help.

Our Mission, Vision & Values

Mission

To deliver high-quality services that support independence, health and wellbeing in our community. We aim to do this in a culturally appropriate and flexible way that respects individual choices and meets people's needs. We value our staff and remain committed to adapting to the evolving needs of our diverse community.

Vision

To promote the highest quality care through education and continuous improvement, in a culturally sensitive and safe environment of service, incorporating principles of informed choice and control, flexibility, confidentiality, respect and social inclusion that will benefit clients, families and staff.

Values

Our values set the standards of behaviour and attitude expected from our staff and volunteers. They keep our service safe, positive and high in quality — for our clients and our people alike.

  • CaringCommitted, thoughtful, supportive and considerate
  • Quality ServiceProfessional and continuously improving
  • AccountabilityHaving pride and taking responsibility
  • RespectShowing regard for the rights and beliefs of others
  • TeamworkWorking together to achieve a common goal
  • Open CommunicationImproving understanding through listening and sharing
  • HonestyBeing sincere and truthful
  • EnjoymentAchieving satisfaction in a happy and healthy community

Our Commitment to You

We're here to make sure you are at the centre of your aged care — supported to make decisions about your care in a way that respects your preferences and goals, and helped to stay at home longer while maintaining your independence.

You have the right to

  • Make your own decisions about your own life
  • Have your decisions not just accepted, but respected
  • Get information and support to help you make decisions
  • Communicate your wishes, needs and preferences
  • Feel safe and respected
  • Have your culture and identity respected
  • Stay connected with your community

Standards we work to

Our staff comply with the Australian Government's Statement of Rights, the Strengthened Aged Care Quality Standards and the Code of Conduct for Aged Care. Copies of these documents are included in your Service Agreement, and you can read the Statement of Rights directly at health.gov.au.

The Aged Care Quality Standards, set by the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission, outline what you can expect from your care: to be treated with dignity and respect, involved in decisions about your care, and supported in a way that meets your individual needs and preferences. They also require services to provide safe, high-quality care, employ skilled and compassionate staff, and create a comfortable and inclusive environment. You have the right to give feedback or make a complaint, and we must listen and respond.

How you can help us

Just as we have our responsibilities, we ask you to please:

  • Treat our team with respect, including their right to work in a safe environment
  • Tell us if your support needs change
  • Give us the information we need to create, deliver and review your support plan, so it matches your needs and funding
  • Tell us when you're not happy with your support, so we can improve

Services We Provide

Support workers are matched to you based on their skills, experience, language and compatibility with your preferences. We cannot guarantee the same worker for every visit, but we make every effort to provide regular support workers in line with your preferences. At times a change may be necessary due to annual leave, illness or other unforeseen events.

Domestic Assistance

Everyday housekeeping carried out safely and securely: surface cleaning, sweeping, mopping, vacuuming, washing, folding laundry and ironing.

Not included: forensic cleaning, furniture removal, tasks requiring work at heights, window cleaning, carpet cleaning or curtain cleaning.

Personal Care

Trained staff with the skills and knowledge to support you safely and respectfully with personal care, based on your needs and preferences — including help with bathing, showering, dressing and grooming.

Home Maintenance

We work with garden maintenance providers to help you keep your garden and lawn well maintained, including lawn mowing and basic weeding.

Not included: pest control or rubbish removal.

Social Support & Community Engagement

Group activities, social outings, community events and opportunities to meet others with similar interests — designed to reduce isolation, promote wellbeing and support your independence.

Transport

For clients who no longer drive or prefer not to use public transport: travel to medical appointments, the pharmacy and grocery shopping. A support worker can accompany you to appointments or assist during shopping trips if you wish.

Not included: travel outside your local area (intrastate, interstate or international), and the cost of medications, groceries or household bills.

Allied Health Support Services

Help accessing allied health professionals — audiologists, chiropractors, dietitians, occupational therapists, optometrists, physiotherapists, podiatrists, psychologists and speech pathologists. Your GP can provide referrals, and Medicare may cover a number of visits.

Not included: costs for appointments that fall outside Medicare coverage.

Meal Preparation

Trained staff to support you with preparing meals at home — planning recipes, making shopping lists, and assisting with cooking and storing meals.

Not included: the cost of food, containers, or premade and delivered meals.

Nursing Care

Qualified and experienced nurses providing safe, appropriate care at home. If you cannot travel to hospital or prefer care at home, our nurses can assist with wound care, medication management, pre- and post-operative care, and end-of-life support.

Medication Management

Your doctor should explain what each medicine is for and how to take it. We can help you stay organised — supporting you with medication management, or creating a clear medication list and reminders.

Continence Management

Bladder or bowel control can change with age, and speaking with your GP early is important — many issues can be managed or improved. We can assist with a continence assessment to identify your needs and help you choose and purchase suitable products.

Aids & Equipment

Equipment can support mobility, sleeping, eating and daily activities. Allied health professionals may recommend specific items after assessing your needs, and we can help you find safe, suitable and affordable options.

What your funding cannot be used for

Your package cannot be used to pay for:

  • Groceries or takeaway
  • Rent, mortgage payments or everyday bills
  • Gambling or entertainment
  • Holidays or travel, including funerals
  • Alcohol or tobacco
  • Services or items unrelated to your assessed care needs
  • Medications or items covered by Medicare or the PBS
  • Programs already funded by the Australian Government

Your Support at Home package is focused on supporting your independence, health and wellbeing at home, so funds must be used in line with your support plan.

Other Programs

Clients can choose from a range of exercise activities available with us, and we work with various allied health professionals to provide a personalised program that works towards your health goals. Your Care Partner is happy to answer questions and discuss your activity preferences.

Assessment & Care Planning

  1. My Aged Care refers you to us

    A Care Partner will contact you to arrange a time to visit and introduce themselves.

  2. Your first assessment, at home

    This takes place in your home and may take up to 90 minutes. We may talk about your health, mobility, continence, relationships, cultural and spiritual preferences, nutrition, pain and other areas that help us understand your needs. You can choose not to answer any question you're uncomfortable with.

  3. We build your Care Plan together

    We develop a Care Plan and set goals based on your needs, abilities and preferences. With your permission, we may also speak with your representative to make sure we have a full picture of how best to support you to live independently at home.

  4. We review it with you — at least every 12 months

    Any updates are made in partnership with you and your representative. Reviews are required at least annually, but you are encouraged to contact your Care Partner any time your needs or circumstances change.

Temporary & hospital leave

If you plan to be away from home for an extended period — visiting family, going on holiday or staying in hospital — we can temporarily pause your services until you return. Please let your Care Partner know the dates as early as possible so arrangements can be made on your behalf. Please also report any falls or incidents to your Care Partner so we can maintain accurate records and support your safety.

Security of tenure & ending services

Security of tenure means your services and your status as an active client of GOCSA remain in place unless you request to discontinue, or we determine a need to cease services. You may request to stop services at any time, and we will work with you to cease services on the date you request.

We will cease services if:

  • The level of support available under Support at Home is no longer adequate to support you living at home
  • You tell your Care Partner you no longer wish to receive services
  • You move to a location where we do not provide services
  • Your condition changes so that your needs, as assessed by My Aged Care, can be more appropriately met by another type of service or care — for example, residential aged care
  • You do not meet your responsibilities as described in the GOCSA Service Agreement, for a reason within your control

Understanding Your Funding

The Support at Home (SaH) program

Support at Home is a level of funding that helps older people keep living independently at home. There are eight funding classifications, with classification 8 giving access to the most services. There are also three short-term care pathways:

  • The Assistive Technology and Home Modifications (AT-HM) Scheme
  • The Restorative Care Pathway
  • The End-of-Life Pathway

SaH funding can be used to pay for services such as allied health, clinical care, equipment and modifications to help you achieve your goals. As your provider we coordinate the services and care you need. Your dedicated Care Partner makes sure your services run smoothly, checks in with you to offer support and advice, and works with you to manage your funds through a personalised budget. If your needs increase, we can assist you to access a higher classification of funding.

Important information for Support at Home

  • Your eligibility, classification and approved services are assessed by an aged care assessor through My Aged Care.
  • You can choose the provider that best meets your needs. They will assign a dedicated Care Partner to create a personalised care plan tailored to your allocated services, budget and individual requirements.
  • Individuals receiving support contribute to the cost of their services based on their income and assets, as determined by Services Australia.
  • If your needs change, your Care Partner can organise a review by the aged care assessor to make sure you get the right support. You may also be able to contribute financially to access additional supports — speak to your Care Partner.

Care management

10% of your SaH funding is retained by Services Australia to be used by GOCSA to deliver monthly care management. Care management is the support your Care Partner provides to help you stay safe, healthy and living at home for longer — planning and reviewing your care, managing your budget, and connecting you to other services.

Your Service Agreement

A Service Agreement is a legally binding contract between you and GOCSA. It sets out the services you'll receive, the prices, key dates, your rights, your agreed budget and your support plan.

Your Budget & Statements

Individualised budget

Client-directed care gives you more choice and control over what services are delivered, and when and where. You are provided with a copy of your budget so you have full oversight of your package income and expenditure. The income is made up of the amount allocated to the package, plus any fees and supplements available to you.

You may request an updated budget at any time — just ask your Care Partner.

Statements

Monthly statements are emailed to you and itemise any fees, equipment fees and other costs.

Your Visits

Service times

We will always do our best to meet your preferred days and times. This isn't always possible due to scheduling and staffing, so we may need to work with you to find the closest suitable time. The agreed time is an estimate only. If we expect a delay of more than 30 minutes, our office will contact you. Unexpected factors such as traffic, roadworks, accidents or clients needing extra support can affect a support worker's arrival time, and we appreciate your understanding when this happens.

Cancelling or rescheduling

We will keep you informed about any changes to your services. If a scheduled service needs to be altered, we will contact you as soon as possible to discuss alternatives. There may be times when we cannot provide a service at the agreed time or date, but we will work with you to arrange another suitable time.

Notice period

If you need to cancel or reschedule a service, please let us know at least 24 hours in advance. Cancellations made with less than 24 hours' notice may incur fees.

After hours

We do not provide an after-hours on-call service. For any changes or queries about your care or service, please make every effort to call your Care Partner during office hours, 9:00am – 5:00pm, Monday to Friday. If the matter cannot wait, please call the main office on (08) 7088 0500 and leave a message. Messages are checked in the mornings, and we will return your call if further information is required.

If we can't reach you at a scheduled visit

You need to be present when we provide services in your home. If you need to leave before we finish, our staff member must also leave.

If a support worker arrives for a scheduled visit and you do not answer the door or respond when telephoned, they will first check all avenues for a response:

  • Knock on windows
  • Check the back and front gardens
  • Ask a neighbour if they have seen you or know where you may be

If there is still no response, the support worker will contact your Care Partner, who will ring you. If you do not answer, they may ring a family member or the person you have nominated to be contacted. If there is no response and you have a key safe, the support worker or Care Partner may enter your home using the key — but only if you have given prior permission. If you have not provided a key or access, emergency services will be contacted.

Staff are not permitted to keep personal keys to your home or belongings under any circumstances. Together with your Care Partner, you will be asked to complete a Non-Response to a Scheduled Visit form so there is a clear plan in place if this situation ever occurs.

Safety in Your Home

Your home is our workplace when we're supporting you, and we have a legal obligation to ensure a safe workplace. There are certain tasks our staff cannot do, and some precautions that must be taken.

Our staff cannot

  • Work at heights
  • Perform heavy cleaning — carpets, windows, washing curtains or scrubbing surfaces
  • Lift or move heavy furniture. If an item is not easy to move, we can't move it. Light furniture such as chairs or stools is fine.

Please supply for cleaning

  • Three cleaning cloths — one for the bathroom, one for the kitchen, one for general cleaning
  • Dishwashing detergent
  • Bathroom cleaner
  • Paper towel
  • A lightweight mop and broom
  • Vacuum cleaner, if you have carpet

Indoor work

Support workers provide a variety of services, which may include light to medium housework. Our staff are not professional cleaners — if you require full cleaning services, your Care Partner can assist with referrals. All equipment must be in good working order, and your vacuum cleaner, squeeze mop and bucket must meet Occupational Health & Safety standards. If you have any concerns, please discuss them with your Care Partner.

Using chemicals in your home

We are committed to the safety of our workforce and our clients, so our staff do not use any hazardous products while carrying out their duties. SafeWork Australia recognises various cleaning products as hazardous when they contain measured amounts of ingredients such as ammonia, sodium hypochlorite (bleach), sodium hydroxide (caustic soda) and glycol ether (solvent).

Hot weather

Where the temperature rises above 37°C we need to minimise heat stress for both staff and clients. This may mean modifying some tasks so that you and the support worker spend only limited time outdoors — for example, doing the shopping or hanging washing earlier in the day when ultraviolet rays are less intense. Gardening duties are not undertaken on these high-temperature days. In consultation with you, your Care Partner may reschedule your visit or allocate the time to indoor duties.

Smoking

We respect your choice to smoke, but please do not smoke while our staff are present. A staff member can choose not to work in your home if you smoke.

Infection prevention and control

We take infection prevention seriously to protect both clients and staff, and follow standard infection control practices at all times. We ask staff not to attend work if they are unwell, which may mean an alternate support worker visits you instead. To help keep everyone safe, please notify your Care Partner if you are unwell with an infection that may pose a risk to staff.

Money handling

As part of your support plan, a support worker may assist with shopping or other errands, including paying bills on your behalf. Support workers are not permitted to withdraw cash from a bank using a withdrawal slip, or to use a client's ATM card and PIN. If you have difficulty accessing your money, please contact your Care Partner. All support workers carry a cash receipt book and will provide receipts so that all spending of your money is properly recorded.

Accidental damage

Our staff are careful, but accidents can happen. We won't be responsible for accidental damage caused to fixtures, fittings and equipment in your home during service. Please keep precious items stored away safely.

Conduct, Privacy & Your Agreement

Code of conduct for our staff

We expect all staff to act professionally and in line with our vision, values and purpose. Any concerns or allegations of improper conduct will be investigated. Clients, families and anyone interacting with us should always experience respectful, high-quality service.

Staff carry photo ID and will identify themselves when visiting your home. If you would like to give feedback on your experience, please note the staff member's name along with the date and time, so we can address the matter promptly.

Gift giving

We understand that clients may sometimes wish to give gifts to staff. However, our policy is that staff cannot accept personal gifts. Money must not be offered to staff under any circumstances.

Privacy and confidentiality

We follow our Privacy, Confidentiality & Release of Information Policy to meet our obligations under the Privacy Act 1988. This policy sets out how we collect, store, use and protect your personal information.

During your assessment we explain what information we hold about you and who can access it. We will not share your information with anyone without your consent. Your nominated representative may access your information unless you tell your Care Partner otherwise.

Your Care Partner will discuss the Consent Form with you, including how your information is stored and how it may be shared with relevant professionals or representatives. You may withdraw your consent at any time.

Service Agreement

Under the Aged Care Act, we must provide you with a Service Agreement when you begin receiving services. It outlines your rights, your responsibilities and the services you will receive, in line with the Aged Care Quality Standards and legislative requirements.

You will receive the Service Agreement before services commence, and it must be signed prior to commencement. We review it every year in line with your anniversary date with GOCSA, and you receive a copy during your assessment and at each annual review.

Support with Decisions

Our clients remain the primary decision-makers for as long as they are able. If you need support with decision-making, an advocate or nominated representative — such as someone appointed under an Enduring Guardianship — may assist.

Under the Aged Care Act, you can choose who supports you to make decisions. These people are called registered supporters. A registered supporter can:

  • Communicate your wishes
  • Support you to make decisions
  • Request, access and receive information about you, which may include personal information held by My Aged Care

To register a supporter

Call My Aged Care on 1800 200 422, or speak to your Care Partner.

Feedback & Complaints

We value all forms of feedback and use it to continually improve the quality of our services. Our Feedback and Complaints Resolution Framework gives clients, families, friends and visitors many ways to share their experiences with us.

We welcome

  • Compliments — to help us recognise what we're doing well
  • Suggestions — to guide us in improving our services
  • Complaints or concerns — so we can address issues promptly and fairly

How to reach us

  • A Feedback Form — complete it yourself, or our staff will do it for you
  • Speaking with staff or management
  • Letters, emails or other written formats
  • Email admin@gocsacc.org.au
  • External regulatory bodies

We will always listen and be fair, and you will never be treated differently for speaking up.

We take all complaints seriously and do our best to resolve them quickly. If you want to make a complaint, you can do it yourself or ask someone you trust to assist — a family member, friend, registered supporter, another organisation or a formal advocate.

We'll keep your concerns private. If we believe someone could be in danger or a potential criminal action has taken place, we will need to share certain details with the appropriate people — we must always meet our responsibilities under the Serious Incident Response Scheme (SIRS) requirements of the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission.

Staff will do their best to resolve your complaint. If they need further assistance, they will refer your feedback to the management team. We will stay in regular contact and update you on how the resolution is going.

Advocacy & Interpreting

Advocacy

Advocacy means speaking up for you and supporting your best interests. It can be informal, like a family member helping you, or formal through an advocacy service. Advocacy helps you access the services you need, protects your rights, and ensures you are treated fairly and respectfully.

GOCSA can provide advocacy at your request, helping to ensure your voice is heard, your rights are protected, and you are supported to make informed choices.

The Aged Rights Advocacy Service (ARAS)

ARAS is a community organisation providing non-legal advocacy for clients within aged care. Its Advocacy Program aims to ensure older people and their representatives have access to accurate and timely information about their rights and protections relating to aged care services, and are assisted to access appropriate aged care and community services.

Interpreting services

We recognise that language is essential to wellbeing, culture and identity. It plays a vital role in how our clients connect with their community and how they participate in their care. You have the right to understand and be understood when communicating with us.

We take all reasonable steps to communicate effectively with you. If My Aged Care informs us that you require an interpreting service, your Care Partner will arrange a qualified interpreter to attend your assessments and service reviews. We also welcome your representative or family member to interpret for you whenever they are able, and we work closely with professional interpreting and translation services so that communication is clear, respectful and culturally appropriate.

Surveys

Feedback is an essential part of how we deliver quality, safe and person-centred services. Each year you will receive an annual survey, which helps us understand your experience across specific areas of our service. You may remain anonymous if you choose — but if you would like us to respond directly to your feedback or keep you updated on any follow-up actions, please include your name on the form. We greatly appreciate the time you take to share your thoughts with us.

Safeguards

Mandatory reporting

We have zero tolerance for any form of abuse towards clients, staff, volunteers or visitors at any time. The Serious Incident Response Scheme (SIRS) helps prevent and reduce the risk and occurrence of abuse and neglect.

Any alleged abuse or neglect is reported immediately to the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission, and both internal and external investigations are initiated. It is unlawful to inflict abuse on clients. If a staff member is alleged to be involved, our internal processes ensure they are not working until an investigation has taken place.

All suspected or witnessed abuse or neglect must be reported to the Community Services Manager as soon as it occurs. All staff receive training on their responsibilities to protect clients and on compulsory reporting requirements.

Police checks

We take the safety of our clients and workforce very seriously. All staff are required to hold a current police check. Police checks for aged care staff, volunteers and contractors are a key safeguard designed to protect older people receiving Australian Government–subsidised aged care services.
